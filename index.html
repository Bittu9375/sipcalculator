The Compound Interest Formula

Understanding the mathematics behind your wealth growth

SIP Future Value Formula

FV = P × [(1 + i)n - 1] ÷ i × (1 + i)
FV
Future Value
Total value at the end of your investment period
P
Monthly Payment
Fixed amount you invest each month
i
Interest Rate
Monthly interest rate (Annual Rate ÷ 12)
n
Total Periods
Number of monthly payments (Years × 12)

Worked Example

Given Values:
P = ₹10,000
Annual Rate = 12%
Time = 10 Years
Step-by-Step Calculation:
Step 1: Calculate monthly rate
i = 12% ÷ 12 = 0.01 (1% per month)
Step 2: Calculate total periods
n = 10 × 12 = 120 months
Step 3: Calculate compound factor
(1 + 0.01)120 = 3.3004
Step 4: Apply formula
FV = 10,000 × [(3.3004 - 1) ÷ 0.01] × 1.01
Final Result:
FV = ₹17,23,457
Total Invested: ₹12,00,000 | Returns Earned: ₹5,23,457

Breaking Down the Formula

The Compound Factor

(1 + i)n represents how your money grows exponentially. Each month's returns become next month's principal, creating a snowball effect.

The Division by i

Dividing by the interest rate normalizes the growth, accounting for the fact that higher rates compound faster than lower rates.

The +1 Factor

The final (1 + i) multiplier accounts for the fact that your last payment also earns interest for that month, unlike earlier payments.

Key Insights

Exponential Growth

Returns grow faster in later years as the base amount increases. This is why long-term investors benefit most.

Time Multiplier

Doubling the time more than doubles your returns. The formula rewards patience and consistent investing.

Rate Impact

A small increase in rate significantly impacts final value. 12% vs 15% over 10 years can mean lakhs more.

Frequently Asked Questions

Got questions? We've got answers.

What is a SIP Calculator?
A SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) calculator helps you estimate the future value of your investments when you invest a fixed amount regularly at a given rate of return. It uses the compound interest formula to project your wealth over time.
Is the return rate guaranteed?
No, the return rate in the calculator is an expected or assumed rate. Actual returns from mutual funds and other investments can vary based on market conditions. The calculator provides an estimate based on historical data.
How much should I invest in SIP?
The amount depends on your financial goals and disposable income. A general rule is to invest 20% of your monthly income. Even small amounts like ₹500 can grow significantly over long periods due to compounding.
What is the best time period for SIP?
Financial experts recommend staying invested for at least 5-7 years to see significant benefits. The longer you stay invested, the more you benefit from compound interest and market cycles.
Can I modify my SIP amount later?
Yes, most mutual funds allow you to increase (step-up), decrease, or pause your SIP. You can also start a new SIP with different parameters if your financial situation changes.
